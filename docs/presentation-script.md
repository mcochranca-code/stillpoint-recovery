# Stillpoint Recovery — Presentation Script

_Suggested length: 5–6 minutes. Demo cues are in brackets; do not enter real recovery or journal information during a public presentation._

## Opening — about 40 seconds

“Stillpoint Recovery is a small, local-first companion for people who want a calmer place to pause, reflect, journal, meditate, explore peer-support listings, and notice progress. It is designed around one principle: a recovery date, a streak, or any particular feature is never required. The app does not create an account or make recovery data depend on a server.”

## Today and the new on-ramp — about 60 seconds

“On Today, the headline is ‘A little space for today.’ The new ‘What would help right now?’ panel gives a clear first choice: a quiet practice, a meeting listing, or a few private journal lines. Each option is optional, and all of the existing Today cards and lower quick actions remain available. On a phone, the bottom bar keeps every primary destination, but uses a short visible ‘Meditate’ label while retaining the full accessible name ‘Meditation.’”

[Show Today at desktop width, then a narrow/mobile preview. Point out the three quick-start links and verify the bottom navigation still exposes Today, Progress, Journal, Meditation, Meetings and Library.]

“The recovery counter is also optional. Someone can use reflection, meditation, meetings or journaling without setting a date. Settings includes contrast, text-size, motion and discreet-display controls.”

## A few ways to use the app — about 90 seconds

“The reflection is original Stillpoint content. The NA Just for Today card opens NA World Services' official page rather than copying the reading. Scripture is optional, and the Our Daily Bread card links to the publisher. These choices avoid presenting outside literature as Stillpoint's own.”

[Open Meditation, show the selectable practice and the phase-synced breathing orb. Then open Meetings and show a local filter, an official source link and the optional map control.]

“Meditation offers adjustable, gentle practice sessions. Meetings combines a dated offline snapshot with local filters and source links. The map is not loaded until a person chooses it; Stillpoint does not ask for device location or send filter text to the map. Meeting information can change, so users should confirm details with the official source.”

[Open the Journey Collection and spin a medallion using touch, pointer or keyboard.]

“Milestones are shown as two-sided 3D medallions. They can be explored with reduced-motion support, and the collection reflects locally stored activity. Sharing is user-initiated, with a preview and controls over milestone details.”

## Privacy and limitations — about 70 seconds

“Journals, profiles, preferences, session records and meeting filters live in browser storage. ‘Local-first’ does not mean encrypted: browser data can be inspected by someone with access to the device or removed if site data is cleared. JSON backups are normal files, so they need careful handling. External publishers and meeting/map sites may receive ordinary connection metadata when opened.”

“Daily meditation reminder settings and meeting-recurrence calculations are present, but meeting-card controls and a complete notification dispatch loop are not finished. Permission is only requested through an explicit Settings opt-in, and delivery is best-effort; browser and device policies can prevent a notification, especially with the app closed. This is a PWA, not a native iOS/Android app.”

## Feedback and close — about 40 seconds

“There is an optional Experience and Usability Feedback form linked from the repository README. It is hosted by Jotform, so responses leave the device; respondents are asked not to provide names, dates, journal entries or diagnoses. The survey's skip wording and required fields still need alignment before wider promotion.”

“The project's next steps are to finish reminders without promising guaranteed delivery, test the mobile-first interface and accessibility behavior, and publish only from the verified source. Stillpoint is a gentle tool for the day someone is in—not clinical care, and not a replacement for professional or peer support.”

## Demo safety checklist

- Use a clean demo profile and fabricated sample data. Never show a real journal, recovery date, trusted contact, backup file or notification.
- Demonstrate the map only after asking the audience and choosing the opt-in button; otherwise leave it unloaded.
- Do not enable a real notification permission on a shared device during a talk.
- Keep the disclaimer visible: local browser storage is not encrypted, reminder delivery is best-effort, meeting schedules can change, and external sites have their own data practices.
